\section{Abstract}
This paper presents the design, development, and deployment of a real-time, scalable industrial safety monitoring system, developed in collaboration with Dawlence Pakistan, to address critical challenges in workplace safety compliance and hazard detection. The system employs advanced computer vision techniques and deep learning models—specifically the YOLOv8 object detection architecture—to identify unsafe behaviors such as missing personal protective equipment (PPE), hazardous postures, and unauthorized zone entries, directly from live CCTV footage.
\\
\\
A major contribution of this work lies in its system-level engineering: the platform is built as an ASGI-based web application that asynchronously transmits real-time safety alerts to clients using WebSockets, ensuring low-latency communication and uninterrupted user experience. The backend architecture is optimized to simultaneously run multiple models on multiple camera streams, using GPU-level parallelism and dynamic resource allocation for maximum throughput. To handle concurrent workloads efficiently, the system employs Python-based multiprocessing to offload tasks such as frame decoding, inference, and result broadcasting into isolated parallel processes, achieving seamless CPU-GPU coordination.
\\
\\
To enhance model performance in real-world local conditions, we collected over 6 hours of surveillance footage from three different CCTV cameras across Dawlence’s Karachi facility and manually annotated the data, resulting in approximately 13,000 labeled instances for each safety-critical class. This annotated dataset included culturally and contextually specific examples, such as women workers wearing red hijabs as part of their protective uniforms. Fine-tuning was performed on top of an already pre-trained SH17(a research to improve YOLOs accuracy in detecting factory related images)-based model. Our fine-tuning significantly improved model accuracy across multiple classes—raising overall mAP50 from 0.119 to 0.643, and improving person detection precision from 0.39 to 0.883.
\\
\\
Further, the application enables real-time video stream ingestion and synchronization from multiple camera sources, supporting live footage streaming and cross-camera event correlation. Designed with horizontal scalability in mind, the architecture allows the integration of an arbitrary number of CCTV feeds without significant performance degradation, making it suitable for large-scale industrial environments.
\\
\\
The development pipeline spans five phases: (1) data collection and annotation from Dawlence’s Karachi facility, (2) deep learning model training and benchmarking, (3) dashboard and backend architecture design, (4) live system integration with on-premise CCTV infrastructure, and (5) rigorous field testing and iterative deployment. Performance evaluations demonstrate high precision in detecting safety violations and effective real-time feedback under production conditions.

This work not only introduces a practical AI-powered safety compliance solution tailored for Pakistan’s industrial sector but also contributes to the broader vision of Industry 4.0 by demonstrating how cost-effective, intelligent, and scalable safety systems can be deployed within existing industrial workflows with minimal infrastructure overhaul.

\section{Introduction}

Pakistan has an employed population of 71.76 million \cite{b1} with a significant portion of the workforce employed in the manufacturing (14.9\%) and construction (14.4\%) sectors \cite{b1}. 
Out of the total workforce population, 1.9 million (2.7\% of total) people faced a work related injury in 2020-2021. Of these injured people, 40.5\% of workers had to consult a medical professional, 11.7\% had to get hospitalized and 31.6\% were forced to take took time off work because of their injury
\cite{b1}. The labour workforce in the industrial sector (engaged in assembly and manufacturing) is one of the most dangerous 
sectors to work in, accounting for 20\% of all workplace injuries in 2020-2021 \cite{b1}.
With the monthly average income in the manufacturing sector being 22,000 PKR \cite{b1}, 
Such injuries can have a significant impact on the financial stability of the workers and their families with many not being able to afford healthcare services at all 
Pakistan stands as one of the countries with the least amount of regulation and protection when it comes to labor rights. For a labor force of 10.63 million (that is part of the formal sector),
Pakistan has nearly 517 labour inspectors which means one inspector for more than 20,560 workers\cite{b3} highlighting the neglect of Labour rights, safety protocols and education of the workers into safe practices.

Furthermore, given these stats, it is not surprising that the construction industry is one of the most dangerous industries to work in.
Although the construction industry accounts for 7\% of all workers, people are employed in the construction industry worldwide. However, 30-40\% of workplace fatalities happen in this Industry. Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA)
in the United States has reported 991 loss of levis in the construction industry since 2006 \cite{muzz8}. Another statistic claims that even though the industrial sector constitutes about 5\% of the workforce in the U.S., fatal injuries in construction 
accounts for about 18\% of all occupational deaths, with the incident rate for nonfatal occupational injuries and illness in construction is 30\% higher than average industries. \cite{muzz8}
\\ 
Similarly, in China 3843 fatal injuries were recorded in 2017 making the construction industry one of the most fatal. Whereas in the United Kingdom, fatalities are also a problem in construction accounting for a total of 29.86\% of all workplace accidents. 
This increasing trend in more frequent exposure to workplace safety hazards is also likely to continue as the dynamic and complex nature of construction projects increase. \cite{muzz8}

\section{The Need for a Solution}
From the number of injuries reported to the worker to inspector ratio, it is clear that workplace 
safety needs to be revamped in Pakistan. Answering how to improve workplace safety (and thus minimize risk and injuries) in an industrial setting, 
we see a multi-prong approach many of which Dawlence (our industrial partner) implements already. \\
Research has found that industries should implement policies and practices that create a supportive environment for safety. This includes proper training, access to personal protective equipment (PPE), 
and clear communication of safety protocols \cite{a1} and collaboration between departments ensures that health promotion and injury prevention efforts are aligned, maximizing their effectiveness \cite{a1}. \\

The specific problems this project intends to target are related to adherence to regulations and supporting data-driven change. Studies found that compliance to local and state workplace regulations as well as 
industry-specific regulations is fundamental to maintaining workplace safety \cite{a1} Safe workplaces are more productive as well. A study found that improved workplace safety directly correlates with increased employee productivity. 
Specifically, firms that implemented comprehensive safety monitoring reported an average productivity increase of 15\% due to reduced absenteeism and fewer disruptions caused by accidents \cite{a3}. 
Enhanced safety measures not only protect workers but also contribute to a more efficient workflow. \\

One way of ensuring workplace safety is through monitoring systems. Monitoring systems can help identify unsafe behaviors and conditions in real-time, allowing for immediate intervention to prevent accidents. 
The frequency of the occurrence of accidents is perhaps the most common indicator of workplace safety and how safe a manufacturing environment 
is \cite{a5} but it should be noted that workplace accidents can only provide an indicator for the absence of safety rather than the presence of it. \cite{a4}
points out that workers who routinely ignore certain safety protocols will often do so precisely because it has yet to result in an accident. Safety related behaviours are a better indicator of safe and unsafe 
behaviour as they are more informative and can be used to measure for the absence of safety as well as the presence of it (unlike the occurrence of accidents as they are the consequences of unsafe behaviour)  
\cite{a4}.  \ The industry can also utilize the data gathered by a monitoring system to inform decisions about workplace safety, which can lead to more effective interventions. Studies 
indicate organizations should regularly collect and analyze data related to incidents, near misses, and employee feedback to identify areas for improvement \cite{a1}  \\ 

This project hence aims to address the issue of workplace safety in the industrial sector by developing a real-time safety monitoring system that can detect unsafe behaviors and conditions in the workplace. 
Such a system can help prevent accidents and injuries by alerting workers and supervisors when unsafe conditions are detected, allowing them to take corrective action \textbf{before} an accident occurs.
The system will use computer vision and deep learning techniques to analyze video footage from CCTV cameras installed in the factory and identify safety hazards in real time.

  \section{Literature Review on Automated Safety Hazard Detection Systems}

  \subsection{Foundational Frameworks for Safety Hazard Analysis}
  Contemporary automated safety hazard detection systems leverage computer vision to replicate human observers' perceptual capabilities through integrated object detection, tracking, and action recognition. As emphasized by \cite{muzz6}, effective frameworks must not only extract visual information but also contextualize it against predefined safety knowledge. This aligns with \cite{muzz8}'s assertion that successful implementations combine deep learning algorithms with rule-based decision-making, particularly for monitoring personal protective equipment (PPE) compliance, hazardous zone exposure, and procedural violations. The field converges on three critical computational tasks: (1) detecting hazard-relevant objects (workers, PPE, machinery), (2) tracking spatial-temporal relationships, and (3) recognizing unsafe actions or procedural deviations.
  
  \subsection{Data Challenges in Construction Safety Monitoring}
  A persistent challenge lies in the scarcity of suitable training data for dynamic construction environments. While advanced implementations like \cite{muzz8} utilize over 100,000 images from 25 construction projects, typical datasets remain limited in scale and diversity. For instance, \cite{h1} uses only 700 samples from a single webcam, and \cite{h3} employs 1,472 crowd-sourced images. These datasets often lack critical characteristics of construction sites, including spatial conflicts, occlusions, multi-scale objects in cluttered backgrounds, and diverse worker poses. To address data scarcity, researchers employ augmentation strategies such as geometric transformations (flips, rotations), photometric variations (hue/saturation adjustments), and synthetic data generation \cite{h2}. Larger datasets like the SHEL5K (5,000 helmet images) and proprietary collections from \cite{a10} (13,300 PPE images) demonstrate improved model generalization.
  
  \subsection{Evolution of Object Detection Architectures}
  YOLO (You Only Look Once) variants have emerged as the dominant architecture for real-time safety monitoring. Comparative studies reveal progressive performance improvements across versions:
  

  \begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \caption{Performance comparison of YOLO variants in safety applications}
    \begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|}
    \hline
    Model & mAP50 & Precision & Recall \\ \hline
    YOLOv8x & 92.9\% & 94.5\% & 86.9\% \\ \hline
    YOLOv5 & 89.1\% & 91.2\% & 83.4\% \\ \hline
    YOLOv3 & 85.0\% & 88.7\% & 79.1\% \\ \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \end{table}

  Key architectural advancements include anchor-free detection in YOLOv8 \cite{h6} and multi-class state detection (e.g., classifying workers as [W, WH, WV, WHV] in \cite{h2}). Notably, \cite{h3} achieves 95\% precision/recall in direct non-compliance detection by training models exclusively on "No Hat" annotations, bypassing traditional multi-object detection pipelines.
  
  \subsection{Spatial Analysis and Hazard Relationship Modeling}
  Accurate spatial reasoning in 2D projections remains a critical challenge. \cite{h1} addresses this through inverse perspective mapping (IPM) with homography matrices, achieving 90\% accuracy in distance estimation. The methodology involves three steps: (1) perspective transformation using known reference points, (2) bounding box coordinate conversion, and (3) Euclidean distance computation in transformed space. For contextual hazard analysis, research diverges into three approaches: heuristic geometric checks (e.g., midpoint-in-polygon tests), probabilistic models like Bayesian networks (67.9\% mAP in \cite{h2}), and end-to-end non-compliance detection (95\% precision in \cite{h3}).

  \section{Project Description}

  \subsection*{Project Objectives}

  The primary aim of this project is to develop and deploy a real-time monitoring system at Dawlence's sheet metal plant in Karachi.
   Our main objectives are:
  
  \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Safety Hazard Detection:} 
      \begin{itemize}
          \item The system should continuously analyse CCTV footage, identifying unsafe behaviours which include ensuring that:
          \begin{itemize}
              \item Workers wear mandatory PPE, including gloves and protective shoes.
              \item Workers avoid active machinery or other restricted areas by monitoring restricted zones.
              \item Workers are not in close proximity to moving machinery, such as forklifts.
              \item Forklifts are not being operated unsafely or in an unapproved manner, which may result in harm.
              \item Safe distance is present between forklifts and workers.
          \end{itemize}
      \end{itemize}
  
      \item \textbf{Alert System for Quick Intervention:} 
      \begin{itemize}
          \item The system should feature an immediate alert/notification system that will inform on-site supervisors of any safety violations. Alerts will be delivered through multiple channels (dashboard, email, SMS).
      \end{itemize}
      
      \item \textbf{Data-Driven Safety Analysis:}
      \begin{itemize}
          \item The system must include a dashboard displaying analytics of safety protocols and their breaches, which Dawlance can later use to identify trends and improve safety protocols and training over time.
          \item Relevant key data must be extracted from Dawlence's CCTV systems for the purpose of fine-tuning the model based on the hazard list provided by Dawlance.
      \end{itemize}
  \end{itemize}
  

  \section{Functional Requirements}

  \subsection*{List of Functional Requirements}
  \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textbf{Forklift Proximity Detection:} Relevant manager(s) should be notified via email and on web application if any worker is standing too close to a forklift.
      \item \textbf{Forklift Speed Estimation:} Relevant manager(s) should be notified via email and on web application if speed of any forklift goes above the company limit.
      \item \textbf{PPE Detection:} 
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item Relevant manager(s) should be able to define Glove Zones by selecting points on a live view
        \item Relevant manager(s) should be notified via email and on web application if any worker is not wearing gloves while working in a glove zone.
      \end{enumerate}
      \item \textbf{Hazardous Area Entry Detection:} 
        \begin{enumerate}
            \item Users should be able to define Hazard Zones by selecting points on a live view
            \item Users should have the ability to activate/deactivate Hazard Zones
            \item Relevant manager(s) should be notified via email and on web application if any worker is entering a hazardous area when activated
      \end{enumerate}
      \item \textbf{Statistics:} The dashboard of the web application should include data visualization of hazard detections made by the system:
      \begin{enumerate}
          \item Total detections made today and total detections made over the course of the month must be displayed.
          \item A pie chart must be displayed to illustrate the distribution of detected hazard types by percentage.
      \end{enumerate}
      \item \textbf{Video Feed:} The real-time annotated footage resulted from the hazard detection model(s) must be viewable for all cameras in the web application.
      \item \textbf{Notification System:} 
      \begin{itemize}
        \item The system will send real-time alerts through the dashboard to the supervisor about detected hazard and a textual description. The notification system shall support alerts via email, and web app notifications.
        \item Users will have the ability to toogle notifications on and off for specific hazards
      \end{itemize}
      \item \textbf{User Management:} The web application should include a user management system with the following features:
      \begin{itemize}
          \item \textbf{User Roles and Permissions:} Allow for different users to be added
          \item \textbf{User Authentication:} Secure login process with multi-factor authentication.
      \end{itemize}
  \end{enumerate}

  \section{Non-Functional Requirements}

  \subsection*{Performance}
  \begin{itemize}
      \item The system should process video and detect safety hazards in real-time with a maximum latency of 5 seconds to allow for immediate alerts.
      \item The AI model shall achieve hazard detection at a minimum rate of 5 frames per second.
      \item The models that the system uses shall be optimized to maintain minimal GPU usage to enable efficient resource allocation and better latency. 
  \end{itemize}
  
  \subsection*{Security}
  \begin{itemize}
      \item All data transmitted between CCTV cameras, the server, and the dashboard shall be done through robust API's to ensure data security and privacy.
      \item User authentication shall be required for dashboard access, with role-based access controls to restrict unauthorized actions (e.g., viewing, managing system settings).
      \item The system shall log all access and modification events in a secure log file to maintain an audit trail of user activities.
  \end{itemize}
  
  \subsection*{Reliability}
  \begin{itemize}
      \item The system shall maintain an uptime of at least 90\%, providing continuous monitoring 24/7 for hazard detection.
      \item The system shall ensure a hazard detection accuracy rate of at least 85\% under standard lighting and visibility conditions.
      \item In the event of system failure or downtime, the system shall automatically restart within 60 seconds to resume monitoring.
      \item A fallback alert mechanism shall be in place to notify administrators if the system encounters a critical error, ensuring timely intervention.
  \end{itemize}
  
  \subsection*{Scalability}
  \begin{itemize}
      \item The system shall support integration of up to 2 additional CCTV cameras to monitor multiple factory zones as required.
      \item The architecture shall be modular to allow deployment in different industrial environments with minimal configuration changes.
      \item The system shall scale horizontally to support additional processing nodes in case of increased demand for real-time analysis across multiple sites.
  \end{itemize}
  
  \subsection*{Usability}
  \begin{itemize}
      \item The web dashboard shall be intuitive, with clear visualization of alerts and hazards, requiring minimal training for floor supervisors.
      \item Real-time alerts shall be designed to minimize false positives, ensuring only critical events trigger notifications. The alerts will be separated into different levels as well.
      \item The system interface shall provide feedback on all actions (e.g., alerts sent, hazard status updates), and include accessible instructions for navigating the interface.
  \end{itemize}
  
  \subsection*{Compliance}
  \begin{itemize}
      \item The system shall comply with Pakistan’s labor and safety regulations, including data protection requirements relevant to employee monitoring.
      \item Video data and personal information shall be stored and managed following data protection standards, with access strictly controlled to authorized personnel.
      \item System usage and data retention policies shall be reviewed periodically to align with any updates to industry safety and privacy regulations.
  \end{itemize}
  
  \subsection*{Data Integrity and Backup}
  \begin{itemize}
      \item All detected hazard events and alerts shall be saved in a tamper-proof database with regular validation checks to ensure data accuracy.
      \item The system shall implement a backup protocol to save video footage, detected events, and user interactions daily, with weekly backups stored securely offsite.
      \item Backup data shall be retained for a minimum of 90 days, after which it shall be securely deleted unless required for compliance or analysis.
      \item In case of data corruption or system failure, backup files shall allow restoration of data within a maximum downtime of 60 minutes.
  \end{itemize}

\subsection{Data Annotations}

The Dawlance Factory environment posed several problems for both the base YOLOv9 and other pretrained models including:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Occlusions} YOLOv9 would not be able to detect people when they were obscured by machienary and other obstacles in the frame
    \item \textbf{CCTV Camera Perspective} Most pretrained models exclusively have eye-level images as their training data. This caused the models to preform poorly on CCTV video footage from a high angle. For example, a Pretrained Model for Forklifts would often mistake a large area of machinery as a forklifts.
    \item \textbf{Absence of Forklift Class} The base YOLOv9 Model did not include a Forklift Class. 
    \item \textbf{Women Workers} Most noteably women workers were being detected considerably less than male workers. This is attributed to the Dawlance Uniform for women workers which included a red Hijab (covering). Pretrained Models would only detect female workers (with very low confidence) when they were facing towards the camera and did not identify them when they faced away from the camera.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Hazard Logic and Function Implementation}

  \subsubsection{Hazard Zone Implementation}
  The hazard zone entry logic is primarily implemented within the \texttt{\_\_processHazardZone} method of the \texttt{Camera} class and the \texttt{HazardZone} and \texttt{Person} classes. We achieved it through the following steps:

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Hazard Zone Definition:} The \texttt{HazardZone} class is initialized with an \texttt{id}, a list of \texttt{roi\_image\_points} defining the polygonal hazard zone in image coordinates, and the \texttt{homography\_matrix} to transform image coordinates to real-world coordinates.
        
        \item \textbf{Person Tracking:} The \texttt{\_\_processHazardZone} method iterates through detected persons (\texttt{r.cls == 6}). For each detected person, a \texttt{Person} object is either created (if it's a new detection) or its coordinates are updated (if it's an existing tracked person) using the \texttt{\_\_update\_tracked\_people} method.
        
        \item \textbf{Zone Containment Check:} The \texttt{IsInZone} method of the \texttt{HazardZone} class uses \texttt{cv2.pointPolygonTest} to determine if the person's feet midpoint lies within the defined image coordinates of the hazard zone.
        
        \item \textbf{Notification:} The \texttt{SetInZone(True)} method of the \texttt{Person} object is called if the person is in the zone. The \texttt{ZoneEntry} method of the \texttt{Person} class checks if the person's state has changed from outside the zone to inside. If so, a notification is generated. \texttt{SetInZone(False)} is called upon the person leaving.
    \end{enumerate}

    \textbf{Distances from Zone:} This is calculated using the \texttt{DistanceFromZone} method of the \texttt{HazardZone} class. It calculates the real-world distance between the person's feet and the closest point on the hazard zone boundary.

    All detections and annotations are then visualized on the frame and added to the \texttt{Queue}.


  \subsubsection{PPE Implementation}
  The PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) detection logic is implemented in the \texttt{\_\_processPPEDetection} method of the \texttt{Camera} class. We have implemented its functionality as follows:

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Detection:} The method iterates through the detection results (\texttt{results[0].boxes}). If the class label is 2 (gloves) or 4 (hand), a bounding box is drawn around the detected object on the frame. If a hand is detected, a notification is generated.

        \item \textbf{Preventing Multiple Notifications:} Hand IDs are added to or removed from the \texttt{\_\_tracked\_hands} set to prevent notifications from being sent in every frame. Since our model may not continuously detect hands in each frame, the \texttt{\_\_tracked\_hands} set is updated by taking the intersection with \texttt{current\_frame\_hands\_ids} to ensure the same hand isn’t added twice.
    \end{enumerate}

  \subsubsection{Forklift Safety}
  The forklift safety features are implemented in the \texttt{\_\_processForklifts} method of the \texttt{Camera} class and the \texttt{Forklift} class. The following functionality has been implemented:

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Forklift Tracking:} The \texttt{\_\_processForklifts} method iterates through detected forklifts (\texttt{r.cls == 1}). For each detected forklift, a \texttt{Forklift} object is created or updated using the \texttt{\_\_update\_tracked\_forklifts} method.
        
        \item \textbf{Person-Forklift Distance:} For each tracked forklift, the method iterates through all currently tracked \texttt{Person} objects. It checks if a person's feet are within the bounding box of the forklift using \texttt{cv2.pointPolygonTest}. If they are, the distance is 0; otherwise, the distance is calculated using \texttt{DistanceBetweenPeopleForklift}, which uses the \texttt{homography\_matrix}.
        
        \item \textbf{Notification:} If this distance is below a predefined threshold (\texttt{config.FORKLIFTPERSONDISTANCE}) and the person is not already in the \texttt{\_\_people\_close\_to\_forklift} list, then a notification is generated and sent. The person is added or removed from the list to ensure that the notification isn't sent on every frame.
        
        \item \textbf{Forklift Speeding:} The \texttt{CalculateSpeedForklift} method calculates the speed based on the position of its bottom midpoint over the last few frames (using previous points \texttt{\_\_prev\_points}) and the frame rate (FPS). If it exceeds a predefined maximum speed (\texttt{config.LIFTMAXSPEED}), then a notification is generated. The forklift is added or removed from the \texttt{\_\_forklifts\_speeding} list to ensure that a notification isn't sent on every frame.
    \end{enumerate}

\subsection{Parallel CCTV Camera Processing}
 The problem of slow video stream processing in the web application was identified, with the video stream stuck at 2 FPS. The YOLO model and DeepSORT tracking were overloading the system. To address this, we implemented several optimization steps:

    
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Switched from Model.track to Model.predict for more efficient tracking.
            \item Reduced frame resolution to 720p, balancing quality and speed.
            \item Replaced DeepSORT with ByteTrack, which is lighter and faster for tracking.
        \end{itemize}
    
        The benchmark results showed significant improvements:
        \begin{itemize}
            \item testYOLO1.py achieved 31 FPS.
            \item The web application, now running three YOLO instances, stabilized at 15 FPS.
        \end{itemize}

We further optimized the system by Inferencing through Nvidia TensorRT backend implemented by Ultralytics library which doubled our overall FPS by achieving around 2x speedup on the core inference task. Our final system can currently reliably process 24+ frames per second. The analytics are as follows:

    def __processHazardZone(self, results, frame):
        #print(f"In processHazardZone : {self.__queue_key}")
        current_frame_ids = set()
        messages = []
        for r in results[0].boxes:
            if r.cls == 6:  # Class 6 corresponds to 'person'
                # Skip if the person is not available or if confidence is low
                if r.id is None or r.conf < 0.3:
                    continue

                #draw person

                            messages.append(notification_message)

                #Processing Distances for people
                    distance_to_zone, closest_point = hazard_zone.DistanceFromZone(person.getFeet())
                    if person.isInZone:
                        distance_to_zone = -distance_to_zone

        self.__remove__tracked_people(current_frame_ids)

            return frame, messages
        except Exception as e:
            print(f"Error processing camera {e}")

        if len(self.__prev_points) == 5:
            self.__prev_points.pop(0)
        self.__prev_points.append(self.__bottom_midpoint) 
        self.__x1, self.__y1, self.__x2, self.__y2 = map(int, coordinates)
        self.__bottom_midpoint = ((self.__x1 + self.__x2) // 2, self.__y2)

    def __calculate_real_distance(self, point1, point2, M):
        point1 = np.array([point1], dtype=np.float32).reshape(-1, 1, 2)  # Shape (1, 1, 2)
        point2 = np.array([point2], dtype=np.float32).reshape(-1, 1, 2)  # Shape (1, 1, 2)
        point_1_real = cv2.perspectiveTransform(point1, M)
        point_2_real= cv2.perspectiveTransform(point2, M)
        distance = np.linalg.norm(point_1_real[0][0] -  point_2_real[0][0])
        return distance

        return self.__calculate_real_distance(self.getBottom(), person1.getFeet(), M)
    
    def CalculateSpeedForklift(self, M, FPS):
        if len(self.__prev_points) <= 3:
            return 0
        else:
            # Calculate the displacement between the current and the third last point
            displacement = self.__calculate_real_distance(self.__bottom_midpoint, self.__prev_points[-3], M)
            time_interval = (1/FPS) * 3
            speed = displacement/time_interval
            return speed

    def __init__(self, id: int, roi_image_points, homography_matrix):
        self.id = id
        self.__roi_image_points = np.array(roi_image_points, np.int32)
        self.__homography_matrix = homography_matrix

    def IsInZone(self, person):
        return cv2.pointPolygonTest(self.__roi_image_points, person.getFeet(), False) >= 0
    
    def gethazardZone(self):
        return self.__roi_image_points
    
    # Function to calculate the distance of a point from a polygon
    def __point_to_polygon_distance(self, point, polygon, M):
        min_dist = float('inf')
        closest_point = None
        for i in range(len(polygon)):
            # Line segment endpoints
            p1 = polygon[i]
            p2 = polygon[(i + 1) % len(polygon)]

            # Find the closest point on the line segment to the point
            line_vec = np.array(p2) - np.array(p1)
            point_vec = np.array(point) - np.array(p1)
            line_len = np.dot(line_vec, line_vec)
            t = max(0, min(1, np.dot(point_vec, line_vec) / line_len))
            proj_point = np.array(p1) + t * line_vec

            # Calculate the pixel distance to the closest point
            dist = np.linalg.norm(proj_point - np.array(point))
            if dist < min_dist:
                min_dist = dist
                closest_point = proj_point

        closest_point_to_return = tuple(closest_point.astype(int))
        point = np.array([point], dtype=np.float32).reshape(-1, 1, 2)  
        closest_point = np.array([closest_point], dtype=np.float32).reshape(-1, 1, 2)  
        
        # Transform the point from which distance is to be found and the point on the polygon closest to the point to the real world
        point_world = cv2.perspectiveTransform(point, M)
        closest_point_world = cv2.perspectiveTransform(closest_point, M)
        distance = np.linalg.norm(point_world[0][0] - closest_point_world[0][0])
        return distance, closest_point_to_return
    
    def DistanceFromZone(self, point):
            return self.__point_to_polygon_distance(point, self.__roi_image_points, self.__homography_matrix)
    def get_homography_matrix(self):
        return self.__homography_matrix
